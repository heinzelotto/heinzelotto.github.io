The mathematics behind ArgMap

An argument map in this project is not a diagram with numbers sprinkled on it. It is a probability model, and the numbers a reader sees are the output of solving that model. This document explains what the model is, how it is solved, what has been proved about it, and — just as importantly — what has not.

It is written for someone who is comfortable with probability but has never seen this project. No prior reading required. Where a claim rests on a derivation, the derivation is cited rather than reproduced; §9 maps every citation to its home document.

On honesty. Every quantitative claim below is either (a) proved, with a pointer to the proof, (b) measured, with a pointer to the script that reproduces it, or (c) explicitly open. The tags [proved], [measured], [standard] and [open] are used consistently and mean exactly that. Section 7 is the full ledger. Two results in this project's history were refuted rather than confirmed; both are stated below, in the places where a reader would otherwise assume the convenient thing.

1. The one-paragraph version

A document declares statements (claims that can be true or false) and evidence lines (reasoning steps between them). Each carries a number the author asserts. The document compiles into a joint probability space over possible worlds, where each authored number the solver takes as input becomes a linear constraint and each strengthed, non-undercutting reasoning step additionally deletes the worlds it rules out. (Both restrictions are real and §3 states them: undercut lines delete nothing, unstrengthed lines are inert, and a check credence is displayed without ever constraining the solve.) The solver then picks the maximum-entropy distribution — the least-committal one — fitting those constraints as well as they can jointly be fit. Every solved number a reader sees is a marginal, or a ratio of marginals, of that one joint distribution; authored values, check credences and the credal band are shown beside them and deliberately are not. Where the authored numbers cannot all hold at once, the solve does not fail: by default each constraint is held by a spring rather than a clamp (§4.2), so the solve settles at a compromise and the residual disagreement is displayed per node as tension.

That last property is the design goal the whole construction serves. An argument map written by a human being will not be globally coherent. The semantics is built so that local honesty is enough, and incoherence becomes a readout instead of an error.

2. Design constraints (why not something off the shelf)

The semantics was designed backwards from authorability. Three constraints drove it, and each rules out an otherwise-obvious choice:

  1. Local elicitability. An author must be able to write a number for one reasoning step while looking only at that step — not at the whole document. This is what makes a large map writable at all.
  2. Survival. A number the author asserts should still be there after solving, unless something else in the document actively contradicts it. An author who writes 0.8 and reads back 0.8636 — the figure the material placement actually produces, measured below — has been silently overruled by bookkeeping.
  3. Local honesty without global coherence. The author is never required to make the document consistent. Inconsistency is a legitimate end state.

These are more restrictive than they look. Constraint 2 in particular [proved] rules out the natural reading in which the authored number is a prior on a rule-activation variable: any such placement makes the achieved conditional depend on the rest of the model, so no document-local number yields a fixed authored conditional (Prop A8, SOLVER_PROOFS §4.8). The same proposition shows the "material implication" placement fails survival outright: on the base case an authored strength of 0.8 reads back as an achieved conditional P(c|a) = 0.8636 [measured] (toy_run.py, case "T2 material hard (regression)", verified 2026-07-29) — the author has been silently overruled by the encoding.

Existing frameworks each give up one of the three. Abstract argumentation (Dung; Pollock) has defeat structure but no calibrated uncertainty. Probabilistic logic programming (Sato's distribution semantics, ProbLog) does give each rule a local number — but as a switch prior, the very placement Prop A8 shows cannot survive solving: the conditional it induces depends on the rest of the model, so constraint 2 fails. Markov logic networks fail constraint 1 instead: a weight is globally normalized and has no document-local meaning. Probabilistic argumentation (Hunter, Thimm) typically stops at abstract graphs. What is new here is not a technique; it is the contract, and the specific compile that satisfies it.

3. The model

3.1 Worlds

The solver works over a joint truth table — the CTT, for conditional truth table — whose coordinates are the statement variables, plus one base switch E₀ for every evidence line that carries a strength (plus compile-internal auxiliaries).

A switch is read as "this rule is in force": a world where E₀ holds is a world where that inference actually carries. Making in-force-ness a random variable, rather than a property of the graph, is what lets a reasoning step be probabilistic without being a mere correlation.

Every authored construct compiles to one or both of:

  • structural zeros — worlds deleted outright, and
  • linear constraints on the distribution over the survivors.

3.2 Evidence lines: the symmetric floor

A support line $E p ψ | π — "ψ, given π, with strength p" — compiles to three things (two carve-outs follow: an undercutting line gets no zero-set, §3.4, and a line with no strength compiles to nothing at all, being display-only):

  1. a base switch E₀;

  2. the zero-set: worlds satisfying E_eff ∧ π ∧ ¬ψ are structurally dead. Where the rule is in force and its premises hold, worlds contradicting its conclusion do not exist. (E_eff is the effective in-force variable of §3.4; absent any undercut it is just E₀.)

  3. the mirror:

    P(E₀ | π) = P(E₀ | ¬π) = p     equivalently   E₀ ⫫ π,  P(E₀) = p

    (SOLVER_SEMANTICS §1.1 writes this as "mirror / floor pair", but GLOSSARY reserves floor pair for the statement device of §3.3. This document uses "mirror" throughout to keep the two apart.)

The mirror is the subtle part, and it is what the constraint of §2.2 forces. Its content is: an inference's in-force rate is a property of the rule, not of whether its premises happen to obtain. Without it, the act of asserting a conditional silently shifts the antecedent's probability by an amount that depends on nothing but bookkeeping. With it, that channel closes exactly: a strength-0 line is provably inert, and the arbitrary reference rate of the switch cancels out of the answer [proved] (CJ1/Prop C8, SOLVER_PROOFS §3.1 — a hand proof; the separately-stated CJ6 residual cancellation over the theory coordinate is the piece that is machine-checked, in lean/ArgmapProofs/Basic.lean).

The number is a floor, not a point value. This is the single most consequential thing to understand about reading a solved map, and the most common source of surprise, so it is worth stating on its own. The line guarantees its conclusion at least this much and says nothing about the rest: where the rule is in force the conclusion holds, and in the remaining worlds the line is simply silent. The reference measure fills that silence, so the achieved conditional lands above the authored strength — at p + (1−p)·½ in the base case. Two things follow. Nothing has been overruled: the rule is in force in exactly the fraction of worlds the author wrote (§2.2's survival constraint is intact, and is checked directly in §5). And evidence can accumulate — two independent lines for the same claim add up instead of one capping the other, which a point value would forbid.

What remains after the mirror is not a bug but a theorem. Asserting a conditional still lowers its antecedent, by a principled and reference-independent amount — the drift tax:

odds(π) = odds_ref(π) × P_ref(ψ|π)^p

This is the Judy–Benjamin phenomenon, familiar from the philosophical literature on conditional constraint updating, arriving here as an exact law [proved]. It is not an artifact to be removed: an author who does want the antecedent pinned can simply author it, and doing so cancels the drift exactly (Adams restoration, Thm C2, SOLVER_PROOFS §6.2 — verified in §5 below).

3.3 Statements: floor pairs and intervals

The primary device for authoring a claim's own credence is a floor pair: a residual mass p⁺ for the statement and p⁻ for its negation, which compile to two premise-less evidence lines and constrain

P(s) ∈ [p⁺, 1 − p⁻]

[proved] (Prop A6, SOLVER_PROOFS §4.6). A single point credence @s p is read as the degenerate coinciding pair p⁺ = p = 1 − p⁻.

Two further slots matter for reading a map:

  • The balancing prior is the reference tilt that selects a point inside a non-degenerate interval. It is named in the ratified model but has no formalization beyond "engine-chosen" and no elicitation practice. On the flagship map this is masked by the authored pins; on maps authored without statement credences it becomes load-bearing. This is the single most significant open item on the authoring side (see §8).
  • The check credence is the author's all-things-considered number, displayed against the computed posterior but never constraining the solve. It is how a map can say "the argument delivers 0.7, and I believe 0.9" without one silently overwriting the other.

Pairs are authored under a residual rule: a statement's own mass should be evidence not already present elsewhere in the map, since double-counting the same consideration is exactly what the accumulation machinery assumes away. Frontier roots are exempt — their residual mass is the map's interface to the unmapped world.

3.4 Undercuts: attacking the inference itself

An objection can attack a claim, or it can attack a step — Pollock's undercutting defeat. The latter is first-class here, because evidence lines are nodes and can therefore be referenced.

A line whose premises carry $X as a positive conjunct and whose conclusion negates X's conclusion is the inhibitor of X. Effective in-force is then defined recursively over the (acyclic) reference graph:

E_eff(X) = E₀_X ∧ ⋀ ¬( γ_L ∧ E_eff(L) )    over all L inhibiting X

where γ_L is the inhibitor's remaining premises (its guard). Inhibitors carry no zero-set of their own; they take a single unconditional constraint on their own operative rate, exactly as §3.2 does for support.

Two structural properties fall out. Undercuts-of-undercuts — reinstatement, in the argumentation literature's sense — are just the recursion, no new machinery. And defeat only ever revives worlds that the target's zero-set had killed; it never kills new ones. This world-revival principle makes a class of pathology impossible by construction rather than by check.

Strength q′ = 0 is exactly inert and q′ = 1 is exact in-context elimination [measured] (the latter immediate from E_eff = E₀ ∧ ¬(γ∧U)); the behaviour between them is monotone [proved for the flat form under the ratified compile], with the general-graph statement [open] (D1, SOLVER_PROOFS §7.1 — and see §7 for the refutation this claim survived).

3.5 Refinement: the same argument at two grains

A reasoning step can be refined — replaced by a finer subgraph that says how it works. The maximally-unfolded map is the canonical model; only leaf evidences carry switches. A refined line's in-force variable is defined by its delivery function: whether the refinement's own lines, composed, actually deliver the conclusion.

Three consequences, all [proved] (SOLVER_PROOFS §8):

  • Folding and unfolding are a readout, never a re-solve. Every folded quantity is a marginal of the same joint distribution, so the numbers cannot change just because a reader collapsed a box (R3).
  • Coarse and fine agree structurally; only their numbers can disagree, and that disagreement is precisely the tension readout (R2).
  • An authored coarse strength on a refined line is not a solve input at all. It compiles as a zero-force spectator observable, compared against the rate the refinement actually delivers (D38). The obvious alternative — carry it as a soft summary constraint — was proposed and then rejected on measurement: it manufactures correlation between the fine switches, which is precisely the double-count the residual rule exists to prevent (SOLVER_REFINEMENT_DELIVERY §4). D51 fixed a related channel where a reference to a refined line resurrected the coarse row, inflating one flagship node by ≈0.106.

4. The solve

4.1 Maximum entropy

Given the live worlds and the linear constraints, the solver picks the distribution maximizing entropy — equivalently, minimizing KL divergence to the uniform counting reference.

The justification is not aesthetic. For inference from a consistent set of linear constraints, maximum entropy is characterized axiomatically as the unique rule meeting a short list of consistency requirements [standard] (Shore & Johnson, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 26(1), 1980; Paris & Vencovská, Int. J. Approx. Reasoning 4, 1990 — the axiomatic case that it is the common-sense completion). Picking some other member of the feasible set requires a reason the document does not contain. Two honest caveats: the Shore–Johnson axioms have been contested and refined in the later literature, and the characterization is for the consistent case — the default solve is the penalized relaxation of §4.2, which is where inconsistent documents actually land. The choice does carry a commitment: entropy is measured against a reference measure, and the uniform one is a semantic choice, deliberately exposed rather than hidden (§8).

Accounting is F1: entropy is measured over the full joint, switches included. Quotient variants that measure only over statements were implemented and are retired to comparison oracles.

4.2 Two modes, and why the penalized one is the default

Authored numbers frequently cannot all hold at once — two conclusive evidences for opposite conclusions is the minimal case. There are two modes:

  • Hard: constraints exact. Infeasible sets are then genuinely infeasible, and the dual problem has no stationary point. The failure is not graceful: on a family of opposing floors with feasibility gap γ, the exact dual's multipliers grow like ln(C/γ) and its Hessian conditioning like Θ(1/γ), diverging as the gap closes [proved, with the constants derived] (Prop B5, SOLVER_PROOFS §5.5).

  • Ridge (the default): each constraint gets a weight w, and the problem becomes a quadratic-penalized max-ent whose Fenchel dual is a ridge-regularized log-partition minimization,

    min_λ  log Z(λ) + Σᵢ λᵢ² / (2wᵢ)

    with multipliers bounded by construction and the solution smooth (C^∞) in the authored numbers, straight through the point where the hard problem breaks [proved] (B1, B3, B4, B5).

This is the mathematical content of "incoherence is a readout, not an error". The penalized problem is always solvable; conflicting authored numbers produce a compromise plus a per-line residual, rather than a failure. And the two modes are continuously connected: as w → ∞ the penalized solution converges to the hard max-ent solution when one exists, and otherwise to the max-ent point of the minimizers of the violation functional [proved] (Thm B6, §5.6) — so the default is a principled relaxation of the exact problem, not a different problem.

The corpus default is w = 400. Softness re-admits a little of the drift the mirror removed, at order 1/w: on T2 the reopened slab gap is −0.0291 at w = 100, −0.0083 at w = 400, and −0.0022 at w = 1600 — decaying to zero as w grows, with P(a) climbing to the hard-limit 1/(1+2^p) = 0.3648 [measured] (factored_followup_probe.py, probe F5, verified 2026-07-29). w = 400 is the floor: the corpus default is chosen so the reopened gap stays around 0.008, and the weight should not go below it.

4.3 Tension

Tension on a node is |authored − solved|: the distance between what the author asserted and what the whole document jointly implies. Rules get two readouts — the authored reliability held by constraint, and the computed effective in-force, which is the Pollockian "is this rule standing, here, given everything attacking it" number.

Tension is the reader-facing payoff of the entire construction. On the flagship map, whose numbers are derived from the book authors' own confidence language by a rubric fixed before any number was assigned, a tension badge is the book disagreeing with itself — not the mapmaker disagreeing with the book.

5. A worked example

The smallest interesting document is one implication with nothing else:

@a
@c
$imp 0.8 @c | @a

No credence on either statement; one reasoning step of strength 0.8. Under the ratified semantics — symmetric floor, F1 accounting — solving gives:

quantity value reading
P(c | a) 0.9000 the achieved conditional, above the floor
P(E | ¬a) 0.8000 the mirror: the rule's rate holds even where its premise fails
P(a) 0.3648 the drift tax (§3.2), exactly 1/(1+2^p)

(Reproduce: cd experiments/solver-prototypes && python3 toy_run.py, case "CJ1 T2 floor-F1 SYM hard". These are the suite's pinned anchors, verified live 2026-07-29.)

Read the case name. The suite also carries a case called plainly "T2 floor-F1 hard", which returns P(a) = 0.3214. That is the legacy asymmetric arm — the pre-mirror compile, retained as a regression contrast. It is not the ratified semantics, and its numbers should never be quoted as such. The symmetric="all" flag is what selects the model this document describes. (An earlier draft of this section made exactly that mistake.)

All three numbers are worth reading carefully, because each is a place where a plausible expectation is wrong.

P(E|¬a) = 0.8 is the mirror, and with it survival. The rule is in force at exactly the authored rate even in the worlds where its premise fails — being in force is a property of the rule, not of whether the premise obtains (§3.2). The number the author wrote is the number the model holds, which is constraint §2.2, and §2 lists two natural alternative placements that both fail it.

P(c|a) = 0.9, not 0.8. The authored strength is a floor, not a point: it guarantees c in at least 80% of the worlds where the premise holds (Prop A4's Q(ψ∧φ) ≥ p·Q(φ)). It buys that by being in force in 80% of worlds, and wherever it is in force the zero-set forces c outright — 100%, not 80%. In the other 20% the line says nothing, and the reference measure fills that silence at ½. So 0.8 + 0.2 × 0.5 = 0.9. This is the F1 fill identity [proved] (SOLVER_PROOFS §6.8), and it is the single most common source of surprise when reading a solved map: achieved conditionals sit above authored strengths, by exactly the unclaimed residual times the reference.

P(a) = 0.3648, not 0.5. The antecedent was never authored, and asserting a conditional taxes it (§3.2). The drift-tax law gives this one in closed form: at a uniform reference, odds(a) = 1 × (½)^0.8, so P(a) = 1/(1+2^0.8) = 0.3648 — the arithmetic §3.2 invites, landing exactly on the measured value. Author the antecedent — add @a 0.5 — and the drift cancels: the suite's "T4 floor-F1 SYM hard (Adams restored)" returns P(a) = 0.5000 with P(c|a) unchanged at 0.9. That is Adams restoration [proved] (Thm C2), verified live 2026-07-29.

6. Computing it

Naively the joint is exponential in the number of statements. In practice it is not, for a structural reason:

  • The dual objective and its exact gradient are computable in O(n_cliques · 2^(w+1)) per iteration by junction-tree calibration, where w is the induced width of the moralized constraint graph [standard].
  • Argument maps are narrow. If deleting c "escaping" cross-references leaves scopes admitting a join tree, then w ≤ (a+1) + c for premise arity a [proved] (Prop E2′, §9.2). Whether real corpora have that shape with small c is an empirical claim — and on this project's corpus the measured induced width runs 1–5 on the small files and 11 on the four 599-variable comprehensive maps [measured] (jt_check.py, run 2026-07-29). The point is the scaling, not the constant: width 11 on 599 variables is a joint of ~2^12 rather than 2^599.
  • Ridge-Newton coordinate updates need only clique-local quantities, so one update touches O(diameter) messages [proved] (E4). This is what makes interactive re-solving feasible.

The shipped implementation is a Rust junction-tree kernel compiled to WASM, running in the reader's browser — no server, and it works offline. A warm re-solve after a reader changes one number is tens of milliseconds [measured] (~21 ms desktop, DECISIONS D48); the 599-variable flagship map solves cold in ~3 s via the Python CLI [measured] (jt_check.py, 2026-07-29 — machine-dependent). The document is block-partitioned, so editing one topic re-solves that topic at its own standalone cost rather than the whole document's.

Correctness is pinned three ways, all reproducible, all re-run 2026-07-29 [measured]: an explicit-joint oracle over 21 corpus files (worst gradient difference 9.99e-16, worst marginal difference 4.42e-08 — jt_check.py); a suite of hand-derived closed-form anchors, 47 cases carrying 55 assertions, of which §5's three are one case (toy_run.py, ending "ALL ANCHORS PASS"); and a differential test between the TypeScript compiler and the Python oracle, worst 5.2e-7 on iabied-unfold and 1.4e-7 on the comprehensive maps against a 1e-6 gate (solver-compile/tests/differential.test.ts).

Two results were refuted, and both matter for how the project describes itself:

  1. Documents are not generative models (Conj C3, refuted 2026-07-16). It is tempting to say that an argument map with fully-authored conditionals is a Bayesian network. That is false in general: a v-structure counterexample has max-ent placing covariance +0.045 between parents where the BN puts zero, at strictly higher entropy. What is proved is the sufficient direction: for forest DAGs (in-degree ≤ 1) with full CPTs and root marginals, max-ent equals the BN (Thm C3′). Necessity is not proved — agreement outside the forest case is not guaranteed but also not excluded, and symmetric two-parent configurations exist where the two coincide. The philosophical payload sharpens rather than vanishes: authorship defuses the Judy–Benjamin problem exactly as far as the authored structure is tree-shaped, and no further.
  2. Undercut strength-monotonicity failed under the pre-2026-07 asymmetric compile — a non-monotone bump traceable to the undercut's own bookkeeping drift. The ratified compile dissolves it (proved for the flat form), but the refutation is why the general-graph claim is still marked open rather than assumed.

Named open problems (the ordering is this document's editorial judgement, not a ranking any source makes):

  • CJ9 — the accumulation-ordering inequality. FKG-type arguments fail because the zero-set-masked Gibbs measure is not log-supermodular; needs a new idea.
  • CJ10 / D1 general — undercut monotonicity on general graphs.
  • C5c / CJ12 — the general floor algebra for mixed premise contexts.
  • B2 bare / CJ11 — well-posedness of the unweighted KL penalty.
  • D2 — reinstatement fixed-point structure on undercut chains.
  • The balancing prior (§3.3) — not a conjecture but a genuine gap: named in the ratified model, unformalized, and load-bearing on credence-free maps. Not on that list, though earlier drafts of this document put it there: the undercut channel split — how defeat divides between suppressing a rule and denying its antecedent. PAPER_OUTLINE §7 states it as an open problem the formalism "exposes but does not decide", and that wording is pre-ratification: the measured splits it cites are from the retired asymmetric compile, and D36 did decide the question by ratifying the factored-A compile (§3.4). It is recorded here as settled, not open.

8. Limitations

Stated plainly, because a reader who finds these on their own will trust everything else less:

  • Statements are binary. No categorical or continuous variables.
  • The reference measure is a choice. Uniform counting is not neutral in any absolute sense; it is a commitment, and results like the drift tax are stated relative to it. The mirror was introduced precisely because the arbitrary part of that choice (the switch rate) was contaminating answers; it now provably cancels. What remains is intentional.
  • The numbers are only as good as the rubric that produced them. On the flagship map they derive from the source authors' own confidence language by a pre-registered rubric. That makes tensions auditable — but a verbal-to-probability rubric is itself a modeling decision, and a different rubric would move most of them. (Not quite all: a handful of residuals sit at role defaults precisely because the source says nothing to score, and a revised rubric would leave those where they are.)
  • The narrowness result is empirical on one corpus. Width ≤ 11 across this corpus is real evidence, and it is not a theorem about argument maps in general — a map with many cross-references between refinement groups would be wider, and nothing prevents one. Note also that the constant moved: it was ≤ 7 before the 2026-07 factored-undercut compile, which adds auxiliary coordinates. The bound (Prop E2′) is what is proved; the constant is a property of the corpus and of the current compile.
  • This is not a truth oracle. The solve says what a set of authored numbers jointly implies. It has no view on whether they are right.

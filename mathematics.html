The mathematics behind ArgMap

An argument map in this project is a probability model. The numbers a reader sees are the output of solving that model, not decoration on a diagram. This document explains what the model is, how it is solved, what has been proved about it, and what has not.

It is written for someone who is comfortable with probability but has never seen this project. No prior reading required. Where a claim rests on a derivation, the derivation is cited rather than reproduced, and §9 maps every citation to its home document.

One honesty note before the tags. The home documents and scripts this page cites live in the project's working repository, which is not yet public. A citation here therefore names the exact place a claim is checked; it is a record you can hold the project to, not a link you can follow today. What you can verify without the repository is the deployed app itself: the solved values and tension readings this document explains are computed live in your browser, on the shipped map, by the same kernel this page describes.

What the tags mean. Every quantitative claim below is either (a) proved, with a pointer to the proof, (b) measured, with a pointer to the script that reproduces it, (c) a constant the record chose and says so, or (d) explicitly open. The tags [proved], [measured], [standard], [convention] and [open] are used consistently and mean exactly that. Section 7 is the full ledger. Two results in this project's history were refuted, and both are stated below, in the places where a reader would otherwise assume the convenient thing. Every number tagged measured was re-run on the shipped solve on the date beside it; a number from the retired reference of §4.4 says so where it appears.

1. The one-paragraph version

A document declares statements (claims that can be true or false) and evidence lines (reasoning steps between them). Each carries a number the author asserts, and since 2026-09-08 (DECISIONS D161) each number has two axes: a value (how likely) and a count (how firmly it is held, in the currency of coin flips). The lines, with their values, compile into a directed probability network, the map's own: each strengthed reasoning step is a coin at its authored rate, where an in-force step's premises hold its conclusion is forced, and where nothing forces a statement the network fills it at one half. The statements' values are targets on that network, each held with its count, and the solver picks the distribution closest to the network that meets the targets as well as they can jointly be met: an I-projection, computed by a ridge dual over a junction tree. Every solved number a reader sees is a marginal of that one distribution, or the posterior rate of one coin. (Two restrictions are real and §3 states them: an undercutting line forces nothing, a line with no strength compiles to nothing, and a check credence is displayed without ever constraining the solve.) Where the author's numbers cannot all hold at once the solve does not fail: the targets are springs, the residual disagreement is shown, and the colour of a node means one thing only, that the solved value has left what its author wrote.

An argument map written by a human being will not be globally coherent, and that last property is what the construction is built for: local honesty is enough, and incoherence becomes a readout instead of an error.

2. Design constraints (why not something off the shelf)

The semantics was designed backwards from authorability. Each of the constraints that drove it rules out an otherwise-obvious choice:

  1. Local elicitability. An author must be able to write a number for one reasoning step while looking only at that step, without holding the whole document in view. This is what makes a large map writable at all.
  2. Survival. A number the author asserts should still be there after solving, unless something else in the document actively contradicts it. An author who writes 0.8 and reads back 0.8636 (the figure the material placement actually produces, measured below) has been silently overruled by bookkeeping.
  3. Local honesty without global coherence. The author is never required to make the document consistent. Inconsistency is a legitimate end state.

Constraint 2 rules out two natural placements of the number. The "material implication" placement fails survival outright: on the base case an authored strength of 0.8 reads back as an achieved conditional P(c|a) = 0.8636 [measured] (toy_run.py, case "T2 material hard (regression)", verified 2026-08-11). And a rule-activation prior left to float with the rest of the model makes the achieved conditional depend on everything else, so no document-local number yields a fixed authored conditional [proved] (Prop A8, SOLVER_PROOFS §4.8). Under the shipped model the rate IS a prior on the step's coin, and it survives for a reason Prop A8's hypothesis excludes: a count row holds the coin at its authored rate with the line's count (§3.2), so the rate is a soft claim the solve honours rather than a free parameter it re-estimates. Prop A8's "cannot survive solving" is a statement about the unheld prior, and it still holds of that.

Existing frameworks each give up one of the three. Abstract argumentation (Dung; Pollock) has defeat structure but no calibrated uncertainty. Probabilistic logic programming (Sato's distribution semantics, ProbLog) does give each rule a local number, as a switch prior, which is the unheld placement just described. Markov logic networks fail constraint 1 instead: a learned weight is globally normalized and has no document-local meaning. Probabilistic argumentation (Hunter, Thimm) typically stops at abstract graphs. Bayesian networks are the nearest neighbour on the model side, and since D161 the map's reference IS one (§3.1): what a Bayesian network lacks is the second half, a way to hold an author's number on a derived node as a soft claim beside the network's own value, which is what the targets and their counts add. The nearest neighbour on the authoring side is probabilistic conditional logic under maximum entropy (Paris 1994; Kern-Isberner 1998, 2001; the SPIRIT and MEcore systems, Rödder et al. 2006, Finthammer et al. 2009; survey: Kern-Isberner, Beierle, Finthammer, Thimm 2011): one conditional with one number as the authoring unit, and an I-projection as the solve. It gives up constraint 3: a conditional there is a hard constraint P(ψ|φ) = d, so the model exists only for consistent knowledge bases, and its number is the conditional probability rather than the inference's reliability (a d below one half is a low conditional there and a weak support here; ideas/plans/semantics-prior-art-kern-isberner-2026-08-31.md §4 carries the translation rule). What is new here is the contract, and the specific compile that satisfies it: the map's own network as the prior, the authored numbers as soft targets with counts, kept well-defined on contradictory maps by the penalty of §4.2.

3. The model

3.1 Worlds, the reference, and the completion principle

The solver works over a joint truth table whose coordinates are the statement variables plus one coin E₀ for every evidence line that carries a strength and a premise (plus compile-internal auxiliaries for undercuts and refinements). A coin is read as "this rule is in force": a world where E₀ holds is a world where that inference actually carries. Making in-force-ness a random variable, rather than a property of the graph, is what lets a reasoning step be probabilistic without being a mere correlation.

The reference the solve starts from is the map's own network (D159, adopted 2026-09-03; shipped by D161, 2026-09-08; GLOSSARY network reference): the statements are grouped into blocks (strongly connected components merged with co-conclusion clusters), and a world's reference mass is, per block, the uniform mass divided by the number of the block's joint values alive under the lines' zero-sets. On the acyclic fragment that is a Bayesian network with uniform fill-ins: P₀(s | parents) = 1 where an in-force line with true premises concludes into s, and ½ otherwise, with each coin's authored rate as its own prior factor. Two things follow at once and are measured in §5: the free antecedent of a lone implication reads 0.5 exactly, and a derived statement reads what its in-force lines deliver.

Every authored construct compiles to one or more of:

  • structural zeros: worlds deleted outright;
  • prior factors: a coin's rate, the block reference;
  • targets: soft rows on the solved distribution, each with a weight derived from its count.

The completion principle is the frame the reference was chosen in (Felix, 2026-08-28; ideas/plans/semantics-bookkeeping-hull-2026-08-28.md section 6, and the project's norm for every new semantics decision since). Every authored number is a marginal or a conditional of ONE complete distribution the map only partly writes down: a statement's pin is the zeroth-order piece, marginalized over everything; a one-premise line the first order; a two-premise line the second. Authoring a node conjures the whole completed structure around it, every expressible sibling at a default value, and the compile is the residue after everything that provably cancels is removed. The question the reference answers is which shape the defaults take: every joint cell at ½ (the uniform counting reference, §4.4) or every unauthored conditional row at ½ along the map's own given-bar direction (the network reference). The two completions differ on exactly one visible thing, the antecedent pull of §4.4, and the network shape is the one whose fingerprint agrees with Bayes (§5, §7).

3.2 Evidence lines: coins with a kind

A support line $E p ψ | π ("ψ, given π, with strength p") compiles to three things (two carve-outs follow: an undercutting line gets no zero-set, §3.4, and a line with no strength compiles to nothing at all, being display-only):

  1. a coin E₀ at prior rate p, a unary factor; an authored 1 is a fact, no coin, a pure zero-set;
  2. the zero-set: worlds satisfying E_eff ∧ π ∧ ¬ψ are structurally dead. Where the rule is in force and its premises hold, worlds contradicting its conclusion do not exist. (E_eff is the effective in-force variable of §3.4; absent any undercut it is just E₀.)
  3. a count row: P(E₀) = p held at weight n / (p (1 − p)), n the line's count in flips, so that a line holding n flips resists a change of its rate like n Bernoulli observations at that rate would. This is the only constraint a line contributes; the mirror rows of the retired reference (§4.4) left the constraint set with D161.

The number is a floor, not a point value. This is the most consequential thing to understand about reading a solved map. The line guarantees its conclusion at least this much and says nothing about the rest: where the rule is in force the conclusion holds, and in the remaining worlds the line is silent. The network fills that silence at ½, so the achieved conditional lands above the authored strength, at p + (1 − p)·½ in the base case: 0.9 for an authored 0.8. This is the F1 fill identity [proved] (SOLVER_PROOFS §6.8; unchanged by the change of reference, because the fill is the reference's own, and re-measured under it in §5). Nothing has been overruled: the coin is in force in exactly the fraction of worlds the author wrote. And evidence can accumulate: two independent lines for the same claim add up by noisy-OR, where a point value would have one cap the other.

The line's count comes from its kind (D161 item 3 and its appendix, the first ruled table; revisable by amendment when a map's acceptance tests say so). The kind is a comment key on the line, # kind: <word>, read off the source's own words, and it is content: it ports 1:1 to a translation. The table, in flips:

kind key count the source's cue
K1a formal formal hard (a line at 1 is a fact; below 1, a row at a million flips) a machine-checked derivation, a universal instantiation, arithmetic
K1b quoted deductive deductive 1000 the source's own "necessarily", "by definition", "it follows"
K2 mechanism mechanism 64 "because", "the process", "any such system"
K3 empirical empirical, optional n= 16, or a larger stated n, never lower "historically", "the record shows", a named count
K4 testimony and judgment testimony 16 "we think", "experts", "our judgment"
K5 analogy and precedent analogy 4 "like", "as with", "the way evolution"
K6 hope and speculation hope 4 "the hope:", "perhaps", "one might hope"
no key 16

The solve reads four counted tiers (1000, 64, 16, 4) and the hard one, so a disagreement between empirical and testimony, or between analogy and hope, moves no number. The constants are [convention]: K2's 64 is continuity with the weight the flagship was authored under (a w = 400 pin at 0.8 was 64 flips), the tiers below it are ratios of four fixed so that a hope tends to bend before an analogy before a mechanism (a tendency by count, never a rule by position: only numbers on the path between a reader's claim and the case's roots can give), and K1b's 1000 is the campaign's amendment A1 of the ruling's 200: at 200, the point cap of §3.3, a quoted deductive line beside two point premises bent with them, and the rationale is now "firmer than any statement a point can be, and softer than a formal step". An undercut takes the kind of its own step, never the kind of the line it attacks.

Measured at the ruled tier, through the shipped compile and the WASM kernel (2026-09-08, D161 item 3; solver-compile/tests/socrates-tier.test.ts pins it as a dated fence). On the confusions map's two-premise Socrates syllogism, both premises at 0.99 and the reader's "not mortal" at 0: formal holds 0.990 while the premises give (0.99 to 0.667 each) and the reader is refused (0.323 against 0); deductive at 1000 reads 0.930, the premises 0.687, the reader 0.303, and the single-premise copy 0.986. The condition the raise was set for (the coin above 0.95 while the premises give) is met by formal and missed by deductive by 0.02 on the two-premise copy; the ladder reads 0.750 / 0.930 / 0.959 / 0.977 / 0.987 at 200 / 1000 / 2000 / 5000 / 20000 flips. The constant stands and the miss is on the record for a ruling.

Kind is content, and it was classified like the values. Two blind passes over the flagship's 348 strengthed lines from the rubric text agreed on 251 (0.721; Cohen's kappa 0.651 against chance 0.201), and a third blind reader broke the 68 ties (D161 appendix item 10). The adjudicated keys on the map, recounted 2026-09-08 by grep: 105 mechanism, 69 deductive, 65 testimony, 45 hope, 34 empirical, 30 analogy, no formal. One line in four got its kind by a judgment call, and a tier is a factor of four in flips; §8 says what that costs.

3.3 Statements: targets with a width-derived count

A statement's authored credence is a pair @s p⁺/p⁻ (D52): p⁺ is the mass the author commits to the statement, p⁻ to its negation, and m = 1 − p⁺ − p⁻ is the silent mass the author declines to place. Under D161 a pair compiles to one target, a row on P(s) at the mixture point p⁺ + m·R(s), where R(s) is the network's own value of s: the author's committed mass, plus the reference's fill of what was left open. A bare point @s p is the coinciding pair, m = 0. On the flagship's 224 pairs every solved marginal sits inside its interval [p⁺, 1 − p⁻] [measured] (2026-09-08 through the compile and the kernel, g-flagship.test.ts; the python route agrees). The alternative, a target allowed to sit anywhere in the interval, was measured and declined: on the same 224 pairs it clips 139 down to p⁺ and 80 up to 1 − p⁻ and moves 207 statements, where the mixture moves 12 (G plan ledger 23, 2026-09-07, a probe).

The statement's count comes from its width (D161 item 2). Walley's imprecise Dirichlet model [standard] (Walley 1996) reads an interval of silent mass m as n = s (1 − m) / m equivalent observations, with s = 2 the prior strength: a 0.7/0.1 pair (m = 0.2) holds 8 flips, a 0.85/0.05 pair (m = 0.1) holds 18, a 0/0.95 pair 38, and a point is read as a pair of silent mass 0.01, 198 flips, the point cap. The cap is count-only (amendment A6): a point stays a point target at its value, the cap sets its weight, and no target moves to a mixture point. The cap is what keeps the compromise of §4.2 alive (no point can make the solve infeasible) and bounds the curvature, so an authored 0 or 1 gets a large finite weight. Both s = 2 and the 0.01 cap are [convention]. The count enters the compile as the per-row weight it already carried, w = n / (p (1 − p)), so a pin at the old w = 400 was already 64 flips at p = 0.8, 36 at 0.9, 100 at 0.5.

On the flagship, recounted 2026-09-08 from argmap-query pinned-roots (224 pairs, no bare point): the rule gives median 18 flips, minimum 0.5, maximum 38 (one pair, @c5-boring 0/0.95); 168 pairs sit at 18, 27 at 8, 25 at 4.67, 3 at 0.5; 55 of the 224 sit below a bare line's 16, and every mechanism (64) and every quoted deductive line (1000) is stiffer than every statement on the map. So under a reader's what-if the authors' own assertions give before their mechanisms do, which is the completion principle's order: a line holds more of the background fixed than a pin.

The two axes are different things: the pair's width says how much the author declines to commit, the count how much it takes to move the author. A claim can be imprecise but firm, or precise but fragile.

Two further slots keep their jobs:

  • The check credence is the author's all-things-considered number, displayed against the computed posterior but never constraining the solve. It is how a map can say "the argument delivers 0.7, and I believe 0.9" without one silently overwriting the other. Since 2026-08-23 it is an interval, # check: 0.85..0.95, the range the authors' register licenses, and the displayed badge is the signed distance from the computed value to that interval, zero inside it. The flagship carries 60 statements with a # check: key on their head line (grep, 2026-09-08; an earlier count of 63 read wrapped gloss lines too), and under the shipped solve 1 of them badges past 0.10 (@evo-analogy, 0.417 against 0.75..0.95, the book's own under every reference; §7 row 4).
  • The balancing prior of the ratified D36 model, the engine's choice of a point inside an authored interval, is no longer a separate slot: the interior point of an interval is the network's own value restricted to it, which is what the mixture point says. Earlier editions of this page listed it as the largest open item on the authoring side; §7 lists it among what closed.

Pairs are authored under a residual rule: a statement's own mass should be evidence not already present elsewhere in the map, since double-counting the same consideration is exactly what the accumulation machinery assumes away. Frontier roots are exempt: their residual mass is the map's interface to the unmapped world.

Premise-less strengthed lines ($E p ψ, a floor with no given bar) are [open] (S18, D161 item 9): the placeholder reads them as targets [p, 1] on the conclusion that pool with their neighbours, which is the reading the one author we have sides with (he-xrisk's check 0.85 met at 0.837 at the K3 default), and a lint note names the placeholder on every such line. No comprehensive map carries one.

3.4 Undercuts: attacking the inference itself

An objection can attack a claim, or it can attack a step, which is Pollock's undercutting defeat. The latter is first-class here, because evidence lines are nodes and can therefore be referenced.

A line whose premises carry $X as a top-level positive conjunct and whose conclusion is the negation of X's conclusion (up to double-negation normalization) is the inhibitor of X, provided exactly one referenced line matches. Near-misses stay ordinary evidence: $X nested under an OR, a conclusion that misses the negation, or an ambiguous line matching two targets all compile as ordinary evidence lines, with flagging left to lint (the shape rule is SOLVER_FACTORED_UNDERCUT §1.1). Effective in-force is then defined recursively over the (acyclic) reference graph:

E_eff(X) = E₀_X ∧ ⋀ ¬( γ_L ∧ E_eff(L) )    over all L inhibiting X

where γ_L is the inhibitor's remaining premises (its guard). Inhibitors carry no zero-set of their own; an inhibitor's coin holds its own operative rate P(U₀) = q′ with its own count. The target's authored rate stays exactly where §3.2 put it: the count row holds the base coin E₀, untouched by defeat, and only the zero-set rides E_eff. An undercut therefore never rewrites the author's claim about the rule's in-force rate; its entire effect is to exempt guard-worlds from the target's zero-set.

Two structural properties fall out. Undercuts-of-undercuts (reinstatement, in the argumentation literature's sense) are just the recursion, with no new machinery. And defeat only ever revives worlds that the target's zero-set had killed. It never kills new ones. This world-revival principle makes a class of pathology impossible by construction rather than by check. The shape is D36's factored-A cascade, unchanged by D161; what changed is the readout: an undercut's coin has a posterior in-force rate like any line, and under a what-if that posterior against the authored rate is the loud row (on the flagship's P6 what-if set the testimony undercut $uc-experts reads 0.542 against its authored 0.30, §3.6).

Strength q′ = 1 is exact in-context elimination (immediate from E_eff = E₀ ∧ ¬(γ∧U)), and q′ = 0 is exactly inert in the hard limit. The behaviour between them is monotone [proved for the flat form under the ratified compile], with the general-graph statement [open] (D1, SOLVER_PROOFS §7.1, and see §7 for the refutation this claim survived).

3.5 Refinement: the same argument at two grains

A reasoning step can be refined, replaced by a finer subgraph that says how it works. The maximally-unfolded map is the canonical model; only leaf evidences carry coins (V8, "refinement replaces", D158). A refined line's in-force variable is defined by its delivery function: whether the refinement's own lines, composed, actually deliver the conclusion.

The consequences, all [proved] (SOLVER_PROOFS §8):

  • Folding and unfolding are a readout, never a re-solve. Every folded quantity is a marginal of the same joint distribution, so the numbers cannot change just because a reader collapsed a box (R3).
  • Coarse and fine agree structurally; only their numbers can disagree.
  • An authored coarse strength on a refined line is not a solve input at all. It compiles as a zero-force spectator observable (D38).

What the folded line shows is open (S21, D161 item 9). The folded readout since 2026-09-08 is D51's composition: the refinement's own lines composed along its spine, shown dimmed and never tinted, on every strengthed refined line the compile can compose (the flagship's nine folded lines, e.g. $link authored 0.93, composition 0.765; 0.856 until the interior premises counted, later the same day, below). The obvious alternative, the whole-network conditional P(conclusion | premises) through the block, was ruled and then reopened the same day (amendment A3), because it reads the conclusion's other parents: on the flagship $resp-waitshot came out at 0.9951, which is @shutdown's marginal, and six of the eight measurable refined lines sat more than 0.05 off their authored strength on the untouched map [measured, 2026-09-08]. That conditional stays a bench observable until a block-local definition exists. The composition reads a refinement recursively: a chain's steps multiply, a convergent group's routes OR, and a fan is the final step AND (any one route with its own interior); the implementation had closed the spine flat until 2026-09-08 (every route's interior at once), which read 0.512 on the flagship's $resp-waitshot where the recursion reads 0.900 against its authored 0.90 [measured, 2026-09-08, W5-A; anchor examples/toys/c-fan.argmap]. What remains open is the product: four steps that are all required multiply to $c5v-resp 0.516 (0.9 x 0.9 x 0.85 x 0.75) on a 0.90 line, and whether that product is the line's own number is S21's remaining question. The composition counts what its steps rest on (W5-G, later on 2026-09-08). A step's coin being on says nothing about whether the step's premise holds, and the event was built from the coins alone, so a hope objection counted as delivering at its full rate beside its own premise at 0.17 ($hope-care 0.673 against 0.15 authored). The readout now reads the DELIVERING event: the coins AND every premise the steps need that nothing inside the refinement delivers, at its posterior, given the coarse line's own premises; what a reference to the line denotes is unchanged (the coins in force), so no marginal moved [measured, 2026-09-08, W5-G: 343 files, both references, max 3.8e-8; anchor examples/toys/c-interior.argmap]. On the flagship: $link 0.856 to 0.765, $hope-care 0.673 to 0.290, $hope-useful 0.220 to 0.074, $hope-harmless 0.243 to 0.245, $hope-design 0.409 to 0.046, $c5-promise-resp 0.810 to 0.644, $resp-waitshot 0.900 to 0.894, $c5v-resp 0.516 to 0.270 (the product times the four steps' own premises), $shutdown-ev 0.864 to 0.777 [measured, 2026-09-08, solve_map.py at the default]. Whether this conditional is the block-local definition is S21's question in its remaining form: it reads the refinement's own event, but its interior at the whole joint's posteriors. Under G without counts the parallel-leaf ladder of P9 is flat (0.1122 at one to four leaves); with counts it returns in proportion to the count, which is S19 (§7).

3.6 The reader's what-if: revision

A reader who moves a statement's number enters it as a point at the point cap, 198 flips, the same rule as an author's point (amendment A2; a reader's 0 or 1 is a point at the cap, never a fact), and it REPLACES the author's number on that statement (fork 4). Two cases follow (D161 item 4 as amended 2026-09-08; the tutorial's 4.6 teaches both with these numbers):

  • On a pinned root, nothing bends and the map follows the reader forward. @llm-nice (the authors' 0.85?/0.05? that current systems seem nice; consumed by two lines, concluded into by none) moved to 0.1: as authored it read 0.877; under the reader's number it reads 0.101, a residual of 0.001 below the tint's tolerance, so nothing colours; the one downstream statement moves (@nat-abs 0.194 to 0.103), the headline stays at 0.827, and no coin sits more than 0.05 from its authored rate [measured] (2026-09-08 through the compile and the kernel, solver-compile/tests/root-whatif.test.ts; solve_map.py --override llm-nice=0.1 agrees).
  • On a derived statement, the author's case retreats where it is softest. The reader's number is one more target beside the author's, the map re-solves, and what bends shows what the case holds least firmly: under the counts a hope tends to bend before a mechanism, a formal step never, the authors' own deductive claims last. On the flagship's P6 set (built, misaligned when built, the misalignment extreme, the link fine, and still nobody dies), the reader's points are met to 0.002 (@everyone-dies 0.0020, @if-built 0.9981, @mis-ext 0.9982), and the retreat lands on the analogy response $resp-exp 0.90 to 0.632, the testimony undercut $uc-experts 0.30 to 0.542, the analogy undercut $uc-precedented 0.30 to 0.351 and one mechanism, $c12dr-obj 0.15 to 0.205; no deductive coin bends [measured] (2026-09-08 through the compile and the kernel, D161 item 4, g-flagship.test.ts). The walkthrough's beat is this case too: the lemma @asi-soon (check-only, 21 lines conclude into it) set to 0.1 reads 0.124, 0.024 refused and tinted, with @everyone-dies following 0.827 to 0.523 and @if-built 0.910 to 0.555 [measured] (solve_map.py --override asi-soon=0.1, 2026-09-08; the editor's walkthrough.test.ts pins the same three through the kernel).

Where the map cannot honour the reader's number, the residual is shown and the row colours past the tolerance of §4.3. The other verb, conditioning (the reader's number as a fact on the author's solved joint; the premises move by Bayes and no line ever gives), answers a different question, "suppose it turned out that way", and differs from revision by 0.5 on the reader's toy (s05: 0.2814 against 0.7910, PLACEMENT_PROBE 20.2, a probe); it stays a bench verb (solve_map.py --condition) and the candidate for a binary mode.

3.7 Explaining away, declared

Two causes of one known effect become dependent in a probabilistic network, and the map, being one, shows it: with only the effect observed both causes rise together, and observing one of them as well lowers the other, because the effect is already accounted for. On the exhibit examples/toys/f-explaining-away.argmap (two causes each bringing the effect at 0.9) the effect alone puts each cause at 0.573, and observing one cause drops the other to 0.515 [measured] (solve_map.py at the default, 2026-09-08; the record's hard-mode figure is 0.5116, against the 0.6552 the conditional-logic literature's syntax-splitting postulate (CInd) would demand). D161 item 6 declares the divergence: the network reference satisfies syntax splitting's (Rel) and (Ind) exactly and (CInd) on fork and chain pivots, and fails (CInd) on a collider pivot by exactly this. (CInd) is not a property the map claims. Under the retired uniform reference the same toy read 0.496 and 0.498: no explaining away at all, which is one more face of the antecedent pull.

4. The solve

4.1 The I-projection

Given the network prior R (§3.1) and the targets, the solver picks the distribution Q that minimizes KL(Q ‖ R) plus the targets' penalties: the I-projection of the reference onto the author's claims. In hard mode (the targets exact) this is Jeffrey's rule for several marginals at once (Williams 1980 [standard]), and it reports INFEASIBLE rather than a number where pins meet zero-sets. For inference from a consistent set of constraints, minimum cross-entropy is characterized axiomatically as the unique rule meeting a short list of consistency requirements [standard] (Shore & Johnson, IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 26(1), 1980; Paris & Vencovská, Int. J. Approx. Reasoning 4, 1990); the earlier edition of this page invoked the same result with the uniform counting measure as R, and what D161 changed is R, not the projection. Picking some other member of the feasible set requires a reason the document does not contain. Two caveats: the Shore and Johnson axioms have been contested and refined in the later literature, and the characterization is for the consistent case, while the default solve is the penalized relaxation of §4.2, which is where inconsistent documents actually land.

Accounting is F1: the divergence is measured over the full joint, coins included. Quotient variants that measure only over statements were implemented and are retired to comparison oracles.

4.2 Two modes, and why the penalized one is the default

Authored numbers frequently cannot all hold at once. The minimal case is two conclusive evidences for opposite conclusions. There are two modes:

  • Hard: targets exact. Infeasible sets are then genuinely infeasible, and the dual problem has no stationary point. The failure is abrupt: on a family of opposing floors with feasibility gap γ, the exact dual's multipliers grow like ln(C/γ) and its Hessian conditioning like Θ(1/γ), diverging as the gap closes [proved, with the constants derived] (Prop B5, SOLVER_PROOFS §5.5).

  • Ridge (the default): each target gets a weight w, and the problem becomes a quadratic-penalized projection whose Fenchel dual is a ridge-regularized log-partition minimization,

    min_λ  log Z(λ) + Σᵢ λᵢ² / (2wᵢ)

    with ln R folded into the static potentials, multipliers bounded by construction and the solution smooth (C^∞) in the authored numbers, straight through the point where the hard problem breaks [proved] (B1, B3, B4, B5; the reference term changes the potentials, not the duality).

What the ridge penalizes deserves one more line, because it is where a reader first meets the solver's arithmetic. The penalty is on each row's moment residual, (w/2)·(E[g] − p·E[h])²; for a target on a statement (h ≡ 1) that is (w/2)·(q − p)², the residual a reader would write. The weight is the count: w = n / (p (1 − p)), so that for small moves the penalty is n·KL(p ‖ q), the price n coin flips at rate p put on a rate q [standard]. What the old single corpus weight (w = 400 for everything) became is therefore not a knob but a table: 198 flips on a point, the Walley count on a pair, the kind's count on a line. Scaling every count by one constant is a temperature against the reference term and not an invariance (on the reader's toy s05, with the reader scaled too, a reads 0.5312 at c = 1/16, 0.5272 at 1, 0.5268 at 1024, PLACEMENT_PROBE 20.2, a probe); what is not saturated is the ratio of counts between the parties to a conflict, which is why the counts are content.

This is the mathematical content of "incoherence is a readout". The penalized problem is always solvable, and conflicting authored numbers produce a compromise plus a per-row residual rather than a failure. The two modes are continuously connected: as the counts grow the penalized solution converges to the hard projection when one exists, and otherwise to the projection's point among the minimizers of the violation functional [proved] (Thm B6, §5.6). So the default is a principled relaxation of the exact problem, and not a different problem.

One price of the retired reference closed with it. Under the mirror rows of §4.4 a conditional row over a thin slab of mass P(φ) was penalized by P(φ)², so a rule over a branch of mass 0.01 could rest 0.24 from its authored value for a penalty near 1e-3 (P7, "the thin-slab price"). With no conditional rows in the constraint set the price has no subject: on the flagship $bounded-escape reads 0.900 against its authored 0.90, where under the retired reference the same line's premise-side row sat at 0.378 [measured] (solve_map.py at the default and at --reference d36, 2026-09-08; D161 item 7 quotes 0.193 for the kernel's effective in-force readout of the same line under the retired reference).

4.3 Tension: the set rule

A statement or line colours only where the solved value has left its authored set (D161 item 5, fork 10): below a floor's value, outside a pair's interval [p⁺, 1 − p⁻], off a point. Tension is the distance from the solved value to that set, zero inside it and below a tolerance of 0.01 (amendment A7, the ridge's own residual at a point: a point at the cap solves about 0.005 off). Inside the set there is no conflict, only the solve filling what the author left open, and that stays visible as a readout (the gauge's fill, a coin's posterior) without colour. On the flagship as authored nothing tints (224 of 224 pairs inside their intervals, every coin within 0.05 of its rate; §3.3), so the tint means exactly "the map could not honour this number". Under a what-if the same rule applies to the reader's own row, which is why the walkthrough's beat of §3.6 colours (0.024 refused) and the root case does not (0.001). A folded refined line is not tinted while S21 is open.

Tension is the reader-facing payoff of the entire construction. On the flagship map, whose numbers are derived from the book authors' own confidence language by a rubric fixed before any number was assigned (D39), a tension badge is the book disagreeing with itself, and not the mapmaker disagreeing with the book.

5. A worked example

The smallest interesting document is one implication with nothing else:

@a
@c
$imp 0.8 @c | @a

No credence on either statement; one reasoning step of strength 0.8. Under the shipped solve (the network reference, ridge, the line at the bare count of 16) solving gives:

quantity value reading
P(a) 0.500 the free antecedent: the network's fill, no tax
P(c | a) 0.900 the achieved conditional, above the floor
P(c) 0.700 0.5 × 0.9 + 0.5 × 0.5
P(E₀) 0.800 the coin at its authored rate

(Reproduce: cd experiments/solver-prototypes && python3 solve_map.py ../../examples/toys/a10-t2.argmap a-free c-free, the toy's ::free block; measured 2026-09-08. The retired reference's reading of the same file is --reference d36: P(a) 0.365.)

Each of the numbers is a place where a plausible expectation is wrong.

P(c|a) = 0.9, not 0.8. The authored strength is a floor, not a point: it guarantees c in at least 80% of the worlds where the premise holds (Prop A4's Q(ψ∧φ) ≥ p·Q(φ)). It buys that by being in force in 80% of worlds, and wherever it is in force the zero-set forces c outright. In the other 20% the line says nothing, and the network fills that silence at ½. So 0.8 + 0.2 × 0.5 = 0.9, the F1 fill identity [proved] (SOLVER_PROOFS §6.8), and it is the single most common source of surprise when reading a solved map: achieved conditionals sit above authored strengths, by exactly the unclaimed residual times the fill.

P(a) = 0.5, and it stays 0.5 whatever the rule says. The antecedent was never authored, and under the network reference asserting a rule about it does not move it: the toy's ::pinned block (the same line with @a 0.5 authored) reads the same 0.500 [measured] (same command; V6, Adams restoration, is the identity here rather than a repair). Under the retired reference the same document taxed a to 0.3648 (§4.4), and §7's row 1 records that the network reference, D159's directed reference and a Dirichlet completion all agree on 0.5000.

The sign of consilience is Bayesian. Give the same ground k consequents, each a 0.8 line and each pinned 0.9 (examples/toys/f-consilience.argmap): the ground reads 0.593 / 0.667 / 0.724 / 0.767 at k = 1 to 4, rising with each confirmed consequence, where the retired reference read 0.464 / 0.432 / 0.402 / 0.375, falling [measured] (solve_map.py at the default and at --reference d36, 2026-09-08). Refutation (f-refutation, the consequents pinned 0.2) falls under both: 0.225 / 0.134 / 0.081 shipped, 0.146 / 0.059 / 0.025 retired. Abduction (a7-abd-pin: a 0.99 rule, the consequent pinned 0.9) reads the ground at 0.600 against 0.450 retired. The plainest reason the reference changed is that first row.

6. Computing it

Naively the joint is exponential in the number of statements. In practice it is not, for a structural reason:

  • The dual objective and its exact gradient are computable in O(n_cliques · 2^(w+1)) per iteration by junction-tree calibration, where w is the induced width of the moralized constraint graph [standard].
  • Argument maps are narrow. If deleting c "escaping" cross-references leaves scopes admitting a join tree, then w ≤ (a+1) + c for premise arity a [proved] (Prop E2′, §9.2). Whether real corpora have that shape with small c is an empirical claim, and on this project's corpus the measured induced width runs 2 to 3 on the toys solved for §5 (2026-09-08), 2 to 6 on the small corpus files under the retired reference (2026-08-11) and 20 on the flagship under the shipped compile (14 under the retired reference, 19 under aref2; pinned 2026-09-08 in solver-compile/tests/reference-timing.test.ts after the composition observable joined moralization) [measured]. What matters is the scaling: width 20 on a thousand-variable map is a largest clique of 2^20 cells rather than 2^1000.
  • Ridge-Newton coordinate updates need only clique-local quantities, so one update touches O(diameter) messages [proved] (E4). This is what makes interactive re-solving feasible.

The route is the one the maximum-entropy expert-system shell SPIRIT took for conditional knowledge bases (Rödder, Reucher, Kulmann 2006: a junction tree of variable clusters representing the global distribution as a product of marginals), applied here to the penalised projection. Loopy belief propagation as an alternative was built into the kernel and measured under the shipped reference on 2026-09-09 and rejected for both the whole solve and a drag tier: its fixed point is five points off on if-built, is not unique on two corpus maps, and returns a different answer after a drag out and back (SOLVER_SCALING-2026-07-16.md §8).

The shipped implementation is a Rust junction-tree kernel compiled to WASM, running in the reader's browser. There is no server, and it works offline. On the keyed flagship under the shipped compile, measured 2026-09-08 on an idle desktop after the campaign's kernel pass (MVP_IMPL_NOTES 2026-09-08, lane W3-C; each timing run alone) [measured]: the compile 759 ms in node, the cold solve 942 ms (14 iterations), and a reader's what-if, the retarget of @everyone-dies to 0, 787 ms; in Chrome 1848 ms cold and 765 ms for the retarget, and 3995 ms / 805 ms with the CPU throttled fourfold, which is not a phone: a real device stays the measurement before a deploy. The retired reference solves the same map in about a tenth of that (cold 102 ms natively), because its tree is narrower; aref2 in about three times it. The document is block-partitioned, so editing one topic re-solves that topic at its own standalone cost rather than the whole document's.

Correctness is pinned three ways, all reproducible [measured]: a parity gate between the TypeScript compile solved in the WASM kernel and the python junction-tree oracle on 162 maps under the shipped reference and 167 under aref2 (the toys, the confusions, the skeptic set; on disk 2026-09-08 in experiments/solver-prototypes/fixtures/g/), worst 1.2e-7 on the shipped reference and one stiff aref2 map at 2e-6 against a 1e-6 gate (solver-compile/tests/g-parity.test.ts, MVP_IMPL_NOTES 2026-09-08); the flagship's acceptance numbers through the compile and the kernel agreeing with the python route (g-flagship.test.ts, root-whatif.test.ts, walkthrough.test.ts; this page's §3.6 numbers were read from both on 2026-09-08); and, for the retired reference, the older triple re-run 2026-08-11 (an explicit-joint oracle over 23 corpus files, worst marginal difference 4.42e-08, jt_check.py; 47 closed-form anchor cases carrying 54 assertions, toy_run.py; the TypeScript-to-oracle differential, worst 1.7e-7 on the comprehensive maps, differential.test.ts).

Why an I-projection in the first place has a literature answer the register cites entry by entry (2026-08-31): Paris (1994, with Vencovská) characterises minimum cross-entropy as the unique inference process satisfying a short list of common-sense principles (equivalence, irrelevant information, obstinacy, renaming, continuity, open-mindedness, ...), and the conditional-logic community holds its inference relations to probabilistic System P and to syntax splitting ((Rel) and (Ind), Kern-Isberner, Beierle, Brewka 2020) and to the kinematics principles descended from Jeffrey ((CaseRel), (CaseIrr); Hahn et al. 2025). V2, V6, V13, V18, V22, V24, V26 and V28 carry those names in parentheses. The network reference satisfies (Rel) and (Ind) exactly and declines (CInd) at colliders by design (§3.7). The two prior-art spokes of 2026-08-31 (ideas/plans/semantics-prior-art-*.md) are the source.

Hard-solve characterisation [proved elsewhere] (read 2026-08-31, ideas/plans/semantics-prior-art-2026-08-31/READS-2026-08-31.md): with one switch atom E per strengthed line and the prior m ⊗ Bern(r), the hard solve is the minimum cross-entropy adjustment to a conditional set per line, so Kern-Isberner (1998) Theorem 11 (the product form), Corollary 12, Theorem 29 and Theorem 31 (the unique adaptation obeying conditional preservation, the functional concept, logical consistency and representation invariance) apply to it verbatim. That reading was made of the D36 solve; under D161 the prior is the network and the targets are its Jeffrey updates, which is the same theorem family with R in place of the counting measure. 8. Limitations

Listed here, because a reader who finds these on their own will trust everything else less:

  • Statements are binary. No categorical or continuous variables.
  • The reference measure is a choice, and the choice is the map's own network with uniform fill-ins (D159, D161). It is directed: writing ~a | ~c instead of c | a is a different network: on §5's document written contrapositively the antecedent a reads 0.300 instead of 0.500 [measured] (solve_map.py at the default on a two-statement toy, 2026-09-08; V15 says exactly this: the given bar stays directional and contraposition is not a rewrite), which is the price of a directed semantics, carried by the authoring tutorial's translation rule. The uniform counting measure was the earlier choice, and §4.4 says what it cost.
  • The numbers are only as good as the rubric that produced them. On the flagship map they derive from the source authors' own confidence language by a pre-registered rubric: its wording-to-number table is on the app's "How ArgMap works" page, and the dated registration record lives in the repository's authoring log (not yet public, like the rest of the repository; the newer maps embed their rubric in the map file itself). That makes tensions auditable. But a verbal-to-probability rubric is itself a modeling decision, and a different rubric would move most of them.
  • The counts are a rubric on top of a rubric. The D39 rubric turns the authors' confidence language into values; the kind rubric turns their reasoning language into counts. One line in four got its kind by a judgment call (kappa 0.651 on the kinds), and the per-kind constants are convention. What the acceptance tests show is that the book as authored is nearly indifferent to the table (one statement past 0.05); what the table decides is the reader's what-if, and there the flagship's own pins, at median 18 flips, give before its mechanisms at 64.
  • The visible flagship holds no root (§7 row 5): the book's numbers are almost entirely the authors' assertions, and the solve fills in between them. A badge on the flagship is therefore the authors' assertions disagreeing with each other through the authors' own steps, and nothing the structure imposes.
  • Explaining away is a feature, and (CInd) is not a property the map claims (§3.7).
  • The narrowness result is empirical on one corpus. Width 20 on the flagship is real evidence, and it is not a theorem about argument maps in general. A map with many cross-references between refinement groups would be wider, and nothing prevents one. Note also that the constant moves with the compile: it was 7 before the 2026-07 factored-undercut compile, 11 under D36 for most of that reference's life, 14 under it today, and 20 under the network reference, whose reference factors and composition observables join moralization. The bound (Prop E2′) is what is proved.
  • This is not a truth oracle. The solve says what a set of authored numbers jointly implies. It has no view on whether they are right.
